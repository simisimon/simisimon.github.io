About Me

Hey! I'm Sebastian Simon, a PhD student at Leipzig University with a strong interest in the configuration space of modern of software systems and AI-driven software engineering.

A key part of my research focuses on analyzing the configuration space of modern software systems to uncover and model configuration dependencies. I also work with large language models (LLMs), retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and agent-based systems to build intelligent tools that help detect and prevent misconfigurations.

Outside of research and development, I enjoy staying active—bouldering, playing badminton, and going for runs are some of my favorite ways to unwind.

Exploring Hyperparameter Usage and Tuning in Machine Learning Research

S. Simon, N. Kolyada, C. Akiki, M. Potthast, B. Stein, N.Siegmund

IEEE/ACM 2nd International Conference on AI Engineering–Software Engineering for AI (CAIN)

This work explores hyperparameter usage and tuning in machine learning research. Our results show that the most of the available hyperparameters remain untouched, and those that have been changed use constant values. Moreover, we found a significant difference between tuning hyperparameters and the reporting about it in the corresponding research papers.

CfgNet: A Framework for Tracking Equality-Based Configuration Dependencies Across a Software Project

S. Simon, N. Ruckel, N. Siegmund

IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering

We propose CFGNET, a framework that models the configuration landscape of a software project as a configuration network in an extensible and artifact-independent way. This way, we enable the early detection of possible dependency violations and proactively prevent misconfigurations during software development and maintenance.
